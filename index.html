01 · The problem

An archive is never neutral

Archives are structured by what an institution defines as “significant”. The National Military Memorial preserves the soldier through granite names, decorations, regiments and decommissioned hardware. The people who make that service possible are only gestured at emotionally — a poem plaque, a general “we owe families a debt” panel — never archived.

Not accidental

The record-keeping categories — postings, ranks, casualties — were never designed to hold domestic or emotional history.

Symbolic annihilation

Caswell’s term: a group’s near-total archival absence works as a statement that their experience does not warrant remembering.

Research question

How does the Memorial’s archival logic exclude military families, and what would an archive built on families’ own authority over their representation look like?

04 · Definition

What is a counter-archive?

In plain terms: a collection built by the people an official archive leaves out, on their terms. In Caswell’s sense, it corrects symbolic annihilation by turning families from backdrop into archival subjects.

    The quiet strength required to keep a household functioning while receiving minimal news during active deployments is completely omitted from national narratives.Spouse of a service member · anonymised survey response

    09 · Why it matters

    Four reasons

    Memory

    Waiting, mourning and domestic labour become part of what the nation is asked to remember.

    Representation

    Caswell: families become archival subjects, not backdrop.

    Authority

    Christen / CARE: the authority to control representation sits with contributors, enforced through consent and access tiers.

    Structural silence

    Stoler: the exclusion is structural, not incidental. The military record was never built to govern domestic labour.
    If an archive shapes who a nation is asked to remember, who should decide what belongs in it?
